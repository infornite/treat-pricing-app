One caveat

Only quotes are formally marked rejected, while sit pending. So pending mixes genuine losses with quotes that merely stalled. The approvals are reliable, the split between dead and dormant is not.

How a price is built

  • Base works rate. The typical core works amount Treat invoiced for that job type, learned from history. The named add on line items are taken out first, so the base is the works only. It is not hours times a rate.
  • Characteristics. Things about the job that make the core works cost more, fitted per type from the history: excavation, difficult access, multiple units, replacement, and after hours. Only drivers the data actually supports for that type are shown, so a wide type like burst pipe resolves to a real number once you say what the job involves.
  • Standard add ons. The fixed line item charges, callout, jet, camera, eel, leak detection, stacked on top the way they appear on the invoice. The ones usual for a job type are ticked by default. Because they were stripped out of the base, adding them here counts them once, not twice. After hours appears in both places on purpose: the works premium sits in the characteristic, the fixed callout fee sits in the add on.
  • No margin, by design. Material cost is blank on most jobs and the labour cost field mirrors the sell price, so there is no cost base. The tool estimates the price, not the margin.
  • Two populations. Reactive is attend and fix, priced after the fact. Quoted works are scoped up front and priced on scope and quantity, so the characteristics and add ons apply to reactive only. The two are not additive.
  • Classification is first pass. Job types come from keywords in the work performed narrative, after stripping recommended future works. The buckets and drivers can be refined as the tool is used.
Sample label. The high, medium and low flag is how much history sits behind a type: high is 40 or more jobs, medium 15 to 39, low under 15. It says how much evidence there is, not how certain the price is.
